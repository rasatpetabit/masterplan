## What actually happened (the honest arc)

The plan said: one release, v10.0.0, through a rehearsed 7-step pipeline. The reality: **seven passes and three corrective cycles**, each teaching the machinery something it assumed but had never lived.

- **Passes 1–3** (10.0.0–10.0.2) died at CI: the gate fixtures needed git identity; host-local PIN EVIDENCE tests had to skip off-host; the doctor's worktree declaration was host-absolute. Each was a real hermeticity lesson the suite now pins.
- **Pass 4** (10.0.3) went green end-to-end through install — and then the walk hit its first true design gap: the branch carried three driver fixes *past the published tag*, and `pr_merge` refused on a precondition no step-receipt machinery could express. A precondition refusal is not a command failure, and the driver had no honest way to say so.
- **The deadlock** was corroborated by a 3-lens finish-time review panel (merge-readiness, branch-correctness, ledger-integrity; verdict rework) before the operator was asked anything. The operator authorized a **recovery-contract amendment**: typed refusal records (`bootstrap_refusal`), evidence-bound reconciliation of doc-only main advances (`main_advance_reconciled`), machine-readable review verdicts (`data.verdict`), plus the gate-anchor fix and the ci_wait exit-boundary correction. It landed as ordinary branch work, closed its own closure review (verdict revise → five findings fixed), and is the reason passes 5–7 could open at all.
- **Pass 5** (10.0.4) exercised the amendment live — and then hit the second design gap: the PR merge conflicted because the branch forked before main absorbed the v9.10.1 release. The spec's own row (retry once, stop) was followed; the operator chose to resolve on the branch and open pass 6 at 10.0.5 — the AUQ answer at the failed-pr-merge gate (ledger event 280 is the failed pr_merge receipt the stop produced; the choice and its options are the session's ask_user_question record).
- **Pass 6** (10.0.5) surfaced the third gap — the first *live* `surfaces_live` tripped a driver probe whose regex expected an output form that never existed; the rehearsal's fixture substitutions had masked it. The operator chose a corrective pass over an in-place validator fix once the record-pinning safeguard (correctly) refused the latter.
- **Pass 7** (10.0.6) carried the probe fix and completed all twelve steps, including the §10.2 gate with its cross-pass audit anchor — the fix the closure review demanded, proven live.

## What the run taught

1. **A refusal is not a failure.** The walk's most consequential gap was vocabulary: precondition refusals (no command ran) and command failures (a receipt exists) needed different records with different honesty contracts. Every recovery after pass 4 rests on that distinction.
2. **One derivation, three consumers.** The expected-base divergence the closure review caught (`recordedMainMover` ignoring reconciliations) was fixed by making it *the* derivation — shared by the refusal evidence, the reconcile range, and the pr_merge precondition — so it cannot drift again.
3. **Rehearsals prove what they run.** Every gap the walk hit live (the conflicted merge, the probe regex) was in territory the rehearsal had substituted with fixtures. The fixtures were honest stand-ins for *shape*, but they validated contracts against forms that never existed in production.
4. **The machinery earns its keep by being usable.** Three corrective cycles sound like failure; in practice each was well-oiled repetition, and every one ended with a cleaner invariant than it started with. The amendment's own fix round was the slow one — two failed delegations (3h09m) before the in-session fix landed, all disclosed in the whole-scope review record — and it is the exception the rule names: even the recovery machinery needed a recovery. The alternative — hand-merges, in-place validator patches, unrecorded steps — was available every time and rejected every time, twice by the operator explicitly.
5. **Reviews must be machine-readable to gate anything.** The pass-5 deadlock had two layers: the review verdict was prose-only (not machine-readable as blocking), and — as the panel established — even a machine-readable blocking review could not bypass the incomplete-predecessor restriction; only the typed-refusal recovery could open the corrective pass. `data.verdict` closed the first layer; the amendment's refusal machinery closed the second.
